Setup

Get connected in 60 seconds

Pair your phone once. After that it finds your PC on its own, even when the PC's address changes.

  1. Start Phone Remote on the PCLook for the orange play icon near the clock (it may hide under the ^ arrow). No icon? Open Phone Remote from the Start menu.
  2. Put both devices on the same Wi-FiThe phone and the PC must share one network. A guest network usually will not work.
  3. Show the pairing codeDouble-click the tray icon to open Phone Remote, then go to Phones. The QR code is there.
  4. Scan it with your phoneIn the Android app tap Scan QR code. Without the app, point the camera at the code and open the link.
  5. Allow the phone on the PCA question appears on the PC: Allow this phone? Choose Allow. You do this once per phone; nobody can join with only the QR code.
  6. Play something on the PCStart a video or song. The title appears on your phone and the buttons come alive.

Next time there is nothing to do. Open the app and you are connected.

Bluetooth needs no software on the PC and works without Wi-Fi. The phone acts as a small Bluetooth keyboard, so you get play, pause, skip and volume, but no track title or progress bar.

  1. Open the Bluetooth tab in the appAllow the Bluetooth permission when asked.
  2. Make the phone visibleTap Make phone visible. It stays visible for two minutes.
  3. Pair from WindowsSettings → Bluetooth & devices → Add device → Bluetooth. Pick your phone and confirm the code.
  4. Connect from the appUnder Paired computers, tap your PC. The status line says Connected.
  5. Use the padMedia buttons work anywhere. The Back and Fwd buttons send arrow keys, so the video player must be the active window.

Still stuck?

The phone says it can't reach the PC

Check that both are on the same Wi-Fi, not a guest network or a VPN. On the PC, make sure Windows calls the network Private (Settings → Network → your Wi-Fi → Private). Then tap the message on the phone to retry.

Windows asked about the firewall

Allow access on Private networks. If you clicked Cancel, run the installer again or allow "Phone Remote" under Windows Security → Firewall → Allow an app.

Nothing shows as playing

Phone Remote sees anything that reports to Windows' media panel: browsers (YouTube, Netflix), Spotify, Media Player and most music apps. Start playback on the PC once, and check the player picker in the top corner if several apps are open.

My video player (VLC, mpv, MPC-HC) shows no progress bar

These players do not tell Windows what they are playing, so by default Phone Remote only sees that they make sound. Fix it once: right-click the tray icon and choose Set up video players. That switches on each player's own remote-control interface (VLC's web interface, mpv's IPC, MPC-HC's web interface). Close the player completely, open it again, and play a video. The title, progress bar and exact seek then work, even while paused.

If the tray item reports a problem for MPC-HC, enable it by hand: View → Options → Player → Web Interface → Listen on port. Players not listed there still get play state, pause, skip and the arrow-key seek fallback.

Skip 10 seconds does nothing in one app

A few apps accept the command and ignore it. Phone Remote notices, and next time it sends arrow keys to that player instead, as long as it is the window in front. See How it works.

I have more than one PC or laptop

Scan each PC's pairing code once. The Android app keeps them all: tap the PC name at the top to switch, add another, or forget one. When a PC's address changes, the app finds it again by its identity, so you never scan it twice. You only need to pair again if you remove that phone on the PC, use Unpair all, or delete Phone Remote's settings.

Remove a phone I no longer use

On the PC, open Phone Remote, go to Phones and press Remove next to it; only that phone loses access. To remove every phone at once use Settings → Unpair all. A phone can also just forget the PC from its own menu.

How do I stop Phone Remote?

It runs in the background until you quit it: right-click the tray icon (near the clock, maybe under the ^ arrow) and choose Quit Phone Remote. Closing a browser tab or a terminal does not stop it. If it ever misbehaves, Open log folder in the same menu shows what it was doing.

The PC's address changed

The Android app finds the PC by name on the network, so this fixes itself. In a browser, scan the pairing QR again.

Under the hood

What happens when you tap play

Your phone sends a tiny message over your home network. The PC passes it to whatever is playing.

Three ways to reach a player

Phone Remote tries them in order and uses the first one that works.

  1. 1 · Windows media sessionThe same "now playing" panel that drives your keyboard's media keys. Apps that appear there can be paused, skipped and seeked precisely, and they report the title and position shown on your phone.
  2. 2 · Check, then retrySome apps say "done" to a seek and do nothing. About a third of a second later we look at the position. If it did not move, we remember that app and use the next method for it.
  3. 3 · Keyboard fallbackFor known players in front of you (browsers, VLC, mpv and similar) we press the Left or Right arrow the right number of times. It only fires when one of those players is the active window, so it never types into your chat app.

Volume and mute use the same system keys as your keyboard, so they change the PC's master volume.

Bluetooth mode, in one line

Your phone pretends to be a keyboard with media keys. Windows needs nothing installed, but the PC cannot send anything back, so there is no title or progress bar.

Privacy and safety

  1. Stays at homeCommands travel only across your local network. The PC refuses connections from outside your home network.
  2. A key per phoneScanning the QR code only lets a phone ask to join, and you must allow it on the PC. Each approved phone then gets its own key, which you can remove at any time. Repeated wrong guesses are locked out.
  3. You stay in controlQuit from the tray icon at any time, or forget a phone from its menu.
